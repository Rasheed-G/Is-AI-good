# Editorial rulebook

**Version 1** (locked 2026-10-29)

The one reference point for what the gallery collects, keeps and shows. The pipeline reads this file: the theme
list, the judge, triage and card copy instructions, and the site's theme summaries all come from here. Change a
rule here, nowhere else. When a rule changes, the version goes up and live stories are re-judged. Each story is
stamped with the version it was judged under.

---

## 1. Purpose
**The gallery is for:**
1. **Showing that AI is causing real harm today**, with sources, across the parts of life our readers care about.
2. **Being trusted.** Every story credible, sourced and calm. Being trusted matters more than being complete.
3. **Not being alarmist.** Show that harms can be challenged, not only that they happen. Say plainly that AI does
   real good and that we focus on what goes wrong.
4. **Leading on.** From today's harms, through the theme pages, towards the bigger risks.

**The gallery is not for:**
- AI doing good on its own. Stories where good and harm sit in the same story are kept.
- General AI news, product launches or AI hype.
- Warnings and predictions about future risk, or lab-only test findings.
- Progress on making AI safe.

---

## 2. Themes
**Reader:** a thoughtful, experienced professional. Topics are never filtered by audience.

**When a story fits two themes:**
1. **The harm decides, not the tool.** A deepfake used to steal money is Scams; to mislead voters, Misinformation;
   a sexual fake, Image abuse.
2. **A harmed child always goes in Kids & safety.**
3. **Bias only when one group is treated worse than others** (including people with a regional accent). A tool that
   fails everyone goes in When AI fails.
4. **"Harm pushed back"** stories (a ruling, a settlement, a law, a company reversing course) and stories mixing
   good and harm go in the theme of the harm. No tag.

| # | Theme | Public summary (shown on the site) | In | Out (goes to) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Jobs & livelihoods** | How AI is changing work, pay and who gets hired. | Job cuts or hiring freezes linked to AI; pay, hours or conditions changed by AI; workers managed or fired by software; hidden AI labour (data labellers, moderators); creative work, voices or writing copied without consent or pay. All kinds of work | A group treated unfairly in hiring → Bias · company AI hype with no effect on people → reject |
| 2 | **Bias & discrimination** | When AI treats people unfairly because of who they are. | One group treated worse than others (race, sex, age, disability, religion, accent) in jobs, care, policing, lending, housing; facial recognition or predictive policing that wrongs a group | A tool that fails everyone → When AI fails · a system deciding about money or rights with no group pattern → Automated decision making · mass monitoring → Surveillance & privacy |
| 3 | **The environment & data centres** | What AI costs the planet and the people living next to it. | Power, water, land, emissions, noise, pollution and bills from AI and data centres; promises to local communities broken | AI used to help the climate, unless the story also shows a harm |
| 4 | **AI and war** | How AI is used in war, weapons and military targeting. | AI in weapons, drones, targeting and military surveillance; harm to civilians; AI in military decisions; military deals only when they show AI actually being used. **Needs independent reporting**, not a government's or army's own claims alone | Predictions about future weapons → not the gallery · AI attacks on government networks with no military link → Hacking · wartime fakes made to mislead → Misinformation. Usually sensitive |
| 5 | **Kids & safety** | How AI puts children at risk, and what's being done about it. | Any harm to under-18s: abuse images of children, sexual or humiliating fakes of children, bullying, chatbots harming children, unsafe products for children | Adults → the theme of the harm |
| 6 | **Relationships & mental health** | How AI chatbots and companions affect people's relationships and mental health. | Adults forming relationships with AI, dependence, isolation, delusions, self-harm encouraged by chatbots, AI "therapy" gone wrong, designs that hook people | Anyone under 18 → Kids & safety |
| 7 | **When AI fails** | When AI fails and people pay the price, in money, health or trust. | Failures with real consequences: made-up facts or court citations, wrong medical or legal advice from chatbots, AI tools that break or delete things, tools that don't work (e.g. policing software with no effect), detectors that wrongly accuse people, self-driving failures, a bad facial-recognition match with no group pattern | People misusing AI on purpose → Scams or Misinformation · AI breaking in or acting beyond its instructions → Hacking · a system deciding about money, benefits, services or rights → Automated decision making · one group failed more than others → Bias |
| 8 | **Hacking & AI acting autonomously** | When AI is used to break into systems, or acts beyond what people told it to do. | Cyberattacks carried out or helped by AI; AI agents taking actions nobody intended **in real systems**; data breaches caused by AI tools or attacks | Warnings about what AI might do, and lab-only test findings (scheming, shutdown resistance) → not the gallery · personal data leaked by a bug → Surveillance & privacy |
| 9 | **Automated decision making** | When a computer decides about your money, benefits, services or rights, with no person checking. | Benefits, insurance, credit, visa, tenancy and care decisions made by systems without human review; wrong denials; fraud flags that hit innocent people; prices set by algorithm (e.g. rent price-fixing) | A group treated worse → Bias · an AI tool's wrong answer that a person or institution then acts on → When AI fails · decisions at work → Jobs |
| 10 | **Misinformation** | How AI spreads false information and fakes that mislead people. | AI-made false content that **spread widely or misled people**: fake news sites and anchors, bot campaigns, AI slop flooding platforms, fabricated images of real events, fake videos or cloned voices of politicians made to mislead voters | A fake that only exists, with no spread or effect → Weak · sexual fakes, politicians included → Image abuse · fakes made to steal money → Scams · one chatbot's wrong answer to one person → When AI fails |
| 11 | **Surveillance & privacy** | How AI watches people and uses their personal data. | Facial recognition and tracking, monitoring of workers or the public, border tracking, personal data collected or used without consent, chats or personal data leaked by a bug | Recognition that wrongs a group → Bias · data stolen in an attack → Hacking |
| 12 | **Scams & fraud** | How criminals use AI to trick people out of money. | Voice-clone calls, deepfake video calls, AI phishing, fake investment ads (including fake politicians or celebrities), AI-boosted identity theft | Fakes made to mislead voters → Misinformation · sexual fakes used for blackmail → Image abuse |
| 13 | **Image abuse** | When AI is used to make fake sexual or humiliating images of real people. | Fake intimate images made without consent (nudify apps), sexual deepfakes of adults, politicians included, sextortion, humiliating fakes of real people, AI influencers using stolen faces | Under-18 victims → Kids & safety · fakes made to mislead → Misinformation · fakes made to steal money → Scams |

**Not themes:** losing skills and thinking, and inequality; weak rules and oversight (a cause, covered on each
theme page).

## 3. Story criteria
### Instant rejects (any one is enough)
1. **No credible source.** No named outlet, court, regulator, study or organisation behind it. A statistic must
   say where it comes from. **A personal account** (a Reddit or social media post about one's own experience)
   counts only when a credible outlet, court or study reports it. Content that is itself AI-generated and can't
   be verified is rejected too.
2. **AI plays no real part.** AI is only mentioned, not used in or causing the harm.
3. **No real event or finding.** Opinion, speculation or "trends" with nothing specific behind it.
4. **Selling something.** Product launches, company PR, funding news, how-to lists, memes, hype or fear marketing.
5. **Politics around AI, not harm from AI.** Who said what, protests, lobbying. Kept when it shows a real harm or
   a harm pushed back.
6. **Headline and story don't match.**
7. **A warning about the future, or a lab-only test finding.**
8. **AI doing good on its own.**
9. Not in English.

### Automatic checks (code, not the judge)
- The source link works.
- A working image or video (no placeholder card).
- A date within 6 months (featured cards, theme-page cards and the owner's stories exempt).
- A theme.
- Not a copy of a story we already have.
- **The card's title and summary describe the same event** (checked after the summary is written).

### Judge scores (each 0 to 100, plus an overall score and a one-line reason)
| Score | The question | About 90 | About 50 | Below 30 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Source credibility** | Can we trust where this comes from? | Major outlet, court, regulator, official data, peer-reviewed study | Trade press or a named site that quotes its sources | Anonymous, or a social post with nothing behind it |
| **Real consequence** | Did someone actually lose something? | Money, a job, health, freedom or safety lost by real people. For a fake: it spread widely or misled people | A likely or limited harm | Inconvenience, or only "could happen" |
| **Wider significance** | Does it show something bigger than one case? | Affects many people, shows a pattern, or a first-of-its-kind ruling | One case of a known pattern | A one-off oddity |
| **Fits our audience** | Would a thoughtful, experienced professional find this credible and worth their time? **Not** "is it about work" | They'd pass it to a colleague | Interesting but thin | Clickbait or trivial |
| **Theme fit** | Does it sit clearly in one theme? | Clearly in one theme's "In" list | Between two themes | Forced |

### The bar: a story goes live when
- no instant reject applies, **and**
- the automatic checks pass, **and**
- the overall score is **at least 70**, **and**
- source credibility is **at least 60**, **and**
- **an unproven claim** (a lawsuit filed, a police referral, an accusation) also has source credibility **at least
  70** and **the harm itself documented** (e.g. tenants did pay more). Otherwise it's Weak. A ruling, settlement,
  charge or conviction counts as proven.

### What the judge can't check
- **Whether the card summary is accurate.** The judge runs before the summary is written: the card copy rules
  (section 7) and the owner's weekly spot-check cover it.
- **Social videos.** The judge sees only a caption. It judges the topic; the owner's spot-check covers the rest.

## 4. Gallery rules
- At most **20 live stories per theme**; when a theme is full, the weakest drops out.
- Stories older than **6 months** leave the gallery.
- **Exempt** from both: featured cards, cards a theme page uses, and the owner's own stories (they skip the
  judge's keep/reject but must pass the automatic checks).
- Stories that age out or drop out are **archived out of Airtable and not shown**.
- Thin themes stay thin. Quality over volume.
- The owner **spot-checks** a sample of live stories each week and pulls weak ones.
- **Sensitive stories** that pass the bar go live **hidden by default, with a label**. Only the riskiest are held
  for the owner (section 6). A held story nobody reviews is **dropped after 4 weeks** and saved to the archive.

## 6. Sensitivity
**A story is sensitive** when it involves children, sexual content, suicide or self-harm, or graphic harm
(violence, injury, death; often AI and war). The judge flags it.

- Sensitive stories that pass the bar go live **hidden by default, with a "Sensitive topic" label**. The reader
  sees them by ticking "show sensitive".
- **Held for the owner only when** the card's picture or video may itself be sexual, graphic or abusive, or a
  child victim could be identified. The judge flags these.
- **A held story nobody reviews is dropped after 4 weeks** (saved to the archive, not shown). Held stories go at the
  top of the owner's weekly spot-check list.

## 7. Card copy
**Reader:** a thoughtful, experienced professional. Not technical, but not new to the world. No jargon; never talk
down.

**Shape:** 2 or 3 short sentences, **at most 50 words**.
1. **What happened:** who, what, where, and the date, exactly as the source gives them.
2. **The consequence, as a fact:** who lost what (money, a job, health, freedom, safety), or what a court,
   regulator or company did about it. A harm pushed back leads with the outcome.
3. **Only if needed, what isn't known or proven:** "the lawsuit is not yet decided", "the researchers can't yet
   prove AI is the cause", "one driver's account".

**Title:** we write our own. One line, about 12 words, the same event as the source headline, calm, our style.
The source headline stays stored.

**Rules:**
- **Say only what the source says.** Keep every number, name, place and date exact; turn "last week" into the
  date; never guess or round.
- **Unproven is labelled as unproven.** A lawsuit "claims", police "say", a study "suggests", as the source does.
- **Name who is responsible** when the source does (the company, the agency, the tool).
- **No stock endings.** Never "this highlights", "this matters because", "this underscores", "raises concerns",
  "serves as a reminder". The consequence sentence does that job.
- **Calm.** No dramatic words ("chilling", "terrifying", "alarming"), no "could" or "may" unless the source says it.
- **Our writing style:** no colons, no em dashes, short sentences, UK English for our own words.
- **Title and summary describe the same event.** If the source headline is about something else (a product
  launch, while the story is about a death), the card is flagged, not patched.
- **Suicide and self-harm:** follow safe reporting (say "died by suicide", never describe the method).
- **Social videos:** describe only what the caption tells us; never invent what the video shows.
- **If the summary can't be written** (the AI fails or refuses), the card is **held, not published**, and the
  summary is retried in the next sweep. After 2 failed tries the story goes to the archive. Raw article text never
  goes on a card.

**Example.** Before: *"On Sunday night, August 16, 2026, Tesla FSD v14.3.6 swerved at 110 km/h on Autoroute 55 in
Quebec's Mauricie region, attempting a missed exit toward a ditch. The driver intervened, but the passenger
screamed in fear. This incident highlights how autonomous driving systems can still pose immediate physical danger
to occupants, even in ideal conditions."* (58 words)
Under these rules: *"On 16 August 2026, Tesla's self-driving software swerved towards a ditch at 110 km/h on a
Quebec motorway. The driver took back control. This is one driver's account, reported by Electrek."* (32 words; it
doesn't add "no one was hurt", because the summary we have doesn't say so)

## 8. Settings
*⚙ = not final: tested against the owner's labelled sample and adjusted if needed.*

| Dial | What it does | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Look-back window | How far back each weekly sweep looks for new items | 7 days |
| Items per source | Most items read from one source per sweep | 25 |
| YouTube results per search | Results per search, per video length | 6 |
| Judge ceiling ⚙ | Most stories the judge reads per sweep | 250 |
| Stories per judge call | Stories sent to the judge together (saves AI usage) | 4 |
| Judge reading length ⚙ | How much of each item the judge reads | about 1,500 characters |
| Best per theme per sweep ⚙ | Most new stories one theme can add in one sweep | 4 |
| Video slot | If none of a theme's best 4 is a video, the best video that passed the bar replaces the 4th | 1 per theme per sweep |
| Pass score ⚙ | Lowest overall judge score that can go live | 70, plus credibility at least 60, and 70 for unproven claims |
| Gallery cap | Most live stories per theme | 20 |
| Age limit | How old a live story can be | 6 months (exemptions in section 4) |
| Held-story expiry | How long a held sensitive story waits before it's dropped | 4 weeks |
| Card length | Summary length | 50 words, plus our own title (about 12 words) |
| Yield windows | Periods used to count each source's kept stories | 10 and 30 days |
| Duplicate check | What counts as a story we already have | Same link; or a title 90% alike, or a summary 72% alike, within 14 days |
| Time limit | Longest a sweep may run | 70 minutes |

## 9. Collection and the filter before the judge
### Triage rules
Triage is a fast, cheap first pass. **It only drops what is obviously not a story for us; when unsure, it keeps**
(the judge decides the rest). If triage fails, everything passes through.

**Drop only if obviously:**
- not about AI at all,
- selling something (product launch, promo, how-to list, meme),
- AI doing good with no harm in sight,
- not in English.

**Never drop at triage** for credibility, consequence, theme or "is it proven": those need the judge. Triage
doesn't see the themes table or the examples (cost).

### Collection
**We read a source when** it brings real events (not opinion or product news), from a credible origin, about at
least one theme.

**Every source has a label:** one of the 13 themes, or **General** (it feeds every theme on purpose: news feeds,
the AI Incident Database, AI subreddits, news channels). The label is for counting; the judge picks each story's
theme.

**Enough is about one kept story a week per theme.** That keeps a theme full under the gallery cap. When a theme
gets less for a month, look for sources. When it gets more, add none: more sources only crowd the judge. AI and war
needs **independent** reporting (monitors, investigative outlets), not a government's or army's own claims; it will
stay thin.

**Retire a source** when it has had plenty of chances and shows nothing (50+ items in 30 days and at most one story
ever), or found nothing in 30 days and never gave a story, or its theme left. Otherwise judge it on how many of its
items the judge read and kept.

**The YouTube keyword gate:** whole-channel YouTube feeds need an AI word in the title. When unsure, the word goes
in: a missed story costs more than a few extra items for triage.

The source list itself lives in Airtable (Sources), not here.
