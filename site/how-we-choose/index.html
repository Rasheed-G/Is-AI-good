How we choose stories

What we look for

Real examples of harm AI is causing, or risks it is creating, right now. Each story has to point to something real, such as a named person, organisation, event or research finding. Every story links to where it came from.

How a story gets here

  1. 1

    Find. We scan news sites, news alerts, YouTube and Reddit for stories about AI.

  2. 2

    Filter. A quick keyword check and an AI tool drop anything off topic.

  3. 3

    Judge. A second AI tool checks that each story points to something real, gives it a theme and a score, and flags sensitive ones. Only the strongest are kept.

  4. 4

    Summarise. AI writes the short summary you see on each card.

  5. 5

    Remove repeats. We drop any story we already have.

  6. 6

    Publish. Most stories go live straight away. Sensitive ones wait until a person has read them.

What we leave out

Hype, statistics with no source, adverts, opinion with no real event behind it, political arguments about AI, and stories where AI is only mentioned in passing.

Sensitive stories

Stories about things like suicide, children or abuse are held back until a person has read them. On the site they stay hidden until you choose to show them.

What this means

Most stories go live without a person reading them first, so mistakes can get through. When we find one, we remove it.

Why it's one-sided

We only collect stories about harm and risk. AI does a lot of good too, and that gets plenty of attention. We focus on the risks because they get the least.
